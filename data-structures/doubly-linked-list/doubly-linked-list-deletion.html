Both Neighbours, No Walk

Deletion

The stored prev pointer is deletion's superpower: the node hands you both its neighbours, so unlinking it is O(1).

Why deletion is easier here

In a singly list, deleting a node means first walking to find the node before it, so its next can be re-pointed — that walk is the O(n) part. A doubly list stores that predecessor already: the victim's own prev field is the node before it, and its next is the node after. Both neighbours are in hand the instant you're standing on the victim, so the unlink itself is O(1).

Route around both ways, then free. Make the victim's predecessor's next skip forward to the victim's successor, and the successor's prev skip back to the predecessor. Now nothing points at the victim from either direction — free it.
  1. Let cur be the victim. Its neighbours are cur->prev and cur->next.
  2. cur->prev->next = cur->next (or move head if the victim was first).
  3. cur->next->prev = cur->prev (or move tail if the victim was last).
  4. free(cur).

 

The two edge cases are the two NULL checks in the code: if the victim was the head it has no predecessor (move head instead), and if it was the tail it has no successor (move tail instead). Handle those and the same routine deletes any node.
