Wire Two Pointers, Not One

Insertion

Every insert is the singly-list move plus one more link — the new node and its neighbour must agree in both directions.

The one rule

In a singly list, inserting means fixing one next pointer. In a doubly list, every place a next points forward, a prev must point back the other way. So the golden rule grows a clause: link the new node to its neighbours (both next and prev), then make both neighbours point back at it. Four pointer writes for a middle insert; fewer at the ends because one side is NULL.

Insert at the head

O(1)
  1. Make the new node; its prev is NULL (it'll be first).
  2. Its next points at the old head.
  3. The old head's prev points back at the new node.
  4. Move head to the new node.

 

Insert in the middle

O(n) to walk, O(1) to splice — four pointers

Walk to the node currently at the target index (call it cur); its predecessor is p = cur->prev, handed to you for free. Then wire all four links:

  1. New node's next → cur, and its prev → p.
  2. p's next → the new node, and cur's prev → the new node.

 

And the tail? Insert-at-tail is the head case mirrored, and because a DLL stores a tail pointer it needs no walk — it's O(1), unlike a singly list. Try it in the playground.
