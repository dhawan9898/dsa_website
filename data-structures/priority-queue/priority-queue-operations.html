Bubble Up, Sift Down

Insert & Extract

Two mirror-image moves keep the heap valid: a new value climbs up toward the root; the root's replacement sinks down.

Overview Insert & Extract Playground

Insert: bubble up

O(log n)

To add a value, drop it in the first free spot — the end of the array, which is the next open leaf. That keeps the tree complete, but the newcomer might be smaller than its parent, breaking the heap rule. So bubble it up: while it's smaller than its parent, swap the two. It climbs until it's no longer smaller — at most one step per level, so O(log n).

  1. Put the value at index n (the end), and grow the heap.
  2. While it's smaller than its parent at (i−1)/2, swap up and follow it.

Extract-min: sift down

O(log n)

The minimum is the root, so removing it is easy to find but leaves a hole at the top. Fill it by moving the last value up to the root (again keeping the tree complete), then sift it down: repeatedly swap it with its smaller child until it's ≤ both children. Swapping with the smaller child is what keeps the parent-≤-children rule true.

  1. Save the root (the answer); move the last value into the root and shrink the heap.
  2. While a child is smaller, swap with the smaller child and follow it down.

Why the smaller child? If you swapped with the bigger child, that child would end up above the smaller one — breaking the rule again. Promoting the smaller child keeps everything below it valid.
