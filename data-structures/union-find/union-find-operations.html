Walk Up, Link Roots, Flatten

Find & Union

Find walks an element up to its root; union links two roots. Two small tricks — union by size and path compression — keep the trees flat.

Overview Find & Union Playground

Union: link the two roots

by size, so trees stay short

To merge two elements' groups, you don't touch the elements — you find each one's root and link one root under the other. If they already share a root, they're in the same group and there's nothing to do. To keep trees short, always hang the smaller tree under the bigger root (union by size).

  1. Find both roots, ra and rb. If equal, they're already one group — stop.
  2. Make ra the bigger of the two (swap if needed).
  3. Point rb at ra, and add rb's size onto ra.

Find: walk up, then compress

the second visit is free

Find walks parent pointers up to the root. On the way back, path compression re-points every node it passed straight at the root — so the next find on any of them is a single hop. It's the trick that turns a possibly-tall tree into a flat one, permanently.

The two tricks compound. Union by size keeps trees from getting tall in the first place; path compression flattens whatever height remains as you query. Together they make every operation cost essentially a constant — the famous inverse-Ackermann α(n).
Union and query in the playground →
Both operations reduce to "walk to a root." Union links two roots (smaller under bigger); find walks up and flattens the path behind it. Neither ever relabels a whole group — that's why it's so cheap.
