    Sorting the edges costs O(E log E); the loop then does at most E finds and V unions, each amortized O(α(V)) with Union-Find — so the whole algorithm is dominated by the sort, O(E log E). A spanning tree on V vertices always has exactly V−1 edges, which is why the loop can stop the instant it has that many.
