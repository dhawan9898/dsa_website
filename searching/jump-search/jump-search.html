Jump in Blocks · Scan the Last One

Jump Search

On sorted data, leap ahead in fixed-size blocks until you overshoot, then walk back through just the last block.

Overview Playground

The idea, in one picture

Flipping through a sorted phone book, you don't read every name and you don't binary-search either — you flip a chunk of pages, check the top name, flip another chunk, and once you've gone past your name you back up and read that one chunk carefully. Jump search is that on a sorted array: leap ahead in fixed blocks of size ≈ √n, checking each block's last element, until that element is ≥ the target. Then linearly scan just that one block.

Why block size √n? With n elements and block size k, you do at most n/k jumps and then k steps scanning a block — n/k + k total. That sum is smallest when k = √n, giving O(√n). It's the sweet spot between one-at-a-time (linear) and halving (binary).

Jump search sits between linear (O(n)) and binary (O(log n)) search. Binary is faster asymptotically, so why ever jump? Jump search only ever steps forward, which matters when jumping back is expensive — e.g. searching data on a medium where seeking backward costs more than reading ahead (tape, some linked structures). On a normal in-memory array, binary search is the better default.
